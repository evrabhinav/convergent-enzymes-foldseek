\begin{abstract}
The DGEB protein classification suite \citep{westroberts2024dgeb}
evaluates amino-acid (AA) foundation models on functional tasks where
sequence homology is wholly or partially removed between train and
test. On Convergent Enzymes Classification (400 Enzyme Commission
(EC) classes, sequence homology removed) the strongest published AA
foundation model, ESM2-3B \citep{lin2023esm2}, reaches a weighted
$F1 = 0.265$; on the easier homology-preserved EC Classification
(128 classes) it reaches $F1 = 0.680$. This work shows that a
confidence-gated combination of Foldseek structural retrieval
\citep{vankempen2024foldseek} and a small protein-LM majority
fallback reaches $F1 = 0.730$ on EC Classification (vs $0.680$) and
$F1 = 0.267$ on the harder, homology-removed Convergent Enzymes task
(vs $0.265$), with no task-specific training and on a CPU laptop with
approximately one hour of free Colab GPU time for the two largest
language-model embeddings. A bootstrap analysis places the DGEB
baseline inside the $95\%$ confidence interval of both results, so the
pipeline matches a 3B-parameter foundation model at a fraction of the
compute rather than beating it. A
per-query analysis on Convergent Enzymes shows Foldseek's per-hit
alignment probability is the dominant separator of easy and hard
queries (Mann--Whitney $p = 3 \times 10^{-5}$).
Negative results are also reported: pocket-geometry features,
discrete 3Di motif counting, and a Nucleotide Transformer v1
ensembling attempt all underperform. Code,
embeddings, and a fully reproducible pipeline are public.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/evrabhinav/convergent-enzymes-foldseek}}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Predicting an enzyme's function from its primary sequence is a
well-studied problem when the test enzyme has a homologous neighbour
in the training set: the most-similar sequence's annotation usually
transfers \citep{gligorijevic2021deepfri,yu2023clean}. The
\emph{convergent} regime, in which enzymes catalyse the same reaction
despite having independently evolved sequences and folds, is much
harder, and only recently has a benchmark been published that
specifically isolates it. The Diverse Genomic Embedding Benchmark
(DGEB) \citep{westroberts2024dgeb} includes a Convergent Enzymes
Classification task with $2000$ training and $400$ test proteins drawn
from $400$ EC classes, with sequence homology removed between train
and test within each class, and an EC Classification task with $512$
training and $128$ test proteins drawn from $128$ EC classes, where
train/test homology is permitted. The DGEB suite evaluates both AA
and nucleotide-track foundation models, but the two classification
tasks examined here are evaluated on AA models only; the strongest
reported AA entry on each is ESM2-3B \citep{lin2023esm2}, at weighted
$F1 = 0.265$ on Convergent Enzymes and $F1 = 0.680$ on EC.

Recent analysis of convergent enzymes \citep{riziotis2025paradigms}
describes three paradigms of functional convergence across a curated
set of M-CSA cases: structural-and-mechanistic convergence, in which
active sites align in 3D even when overall sequence and fold differ
($14$ of the $34$ curated cases, ${\sim}41\%$); mechanistic
convergence without aligned catalytic residues; and reaction-only
convergence. To the
extent that the first paradigm dominates, structural-retrieval tools
that operate on the local 3D environment, such as Foldseek's 3Di
alphabet \citep{vankempen2024foldseek}, should recover much of what
sequence-only models miss, without any task-specific training.

This paper makes the following empirical contributions.

\begin{itemize}
\item On EC Classification, a confidence-gated combination of Foldseek
      structural retrieval with a 4-LM majority fallback
      (ESM2-3B + ProstT5 + ESM2-150M + ESM2-35M) reaches $F1 = 0.730$
      versus the ESM2-3B baseline of $0.680$ (a $+0.050$ point gain;
      every $\tau$-sweep configuration clears the baseline, though a
      bootstrap CI overlaps it, Section~\ref{sec:results-main}).
\item On Convergent Enzymes, the same recipe with a 3-LM majority
      fallback (ESM2-3B + ProstT5 + ESM2-150M) reaches $F1 = 0.267$,
      matching the AA foundation-model baseline ($0.265$) with no
      training and a single AlphaFold structure per protein.
\item To the author's knowledge, this is the first reported Foldseek
      score on either of these DGEB classification tasks: $F1 = 0.238$
      (top-1) on Convergent Enzymes and $F1 = 0.663$ on EC.
\item A per-query analysis on Convergent Enzymes identifies what makes
      queries hard for the AA-track ensemble: Foldseek's per-hit
      probability dominates as a separator ($p = 3 \times 10^{-5}$),
      with coding-sequence (CDS) length and protein length weaker,
      uncorrected effects.
\item Documented negative results: amino-acid sequence features
      (424-D) score $F1 = 0.016$; hand-crafted global structural
      features (100-D) score $0.037$; pocket-geometry features
      (fpocket, 81-D) score $0.019$; discrete 3Di motif counting
      (linear k-mers and spatial pairs/triples) scores
      $\leq 0.073$; Nucleotide Transformer v1 2.5B embeddings in this
      pipeline score $0.008$ alone and reduce the ensemble F1 when
      added.
\end{itemize}

The contribution is to characterise the AA-foundation-model ceiling
empirically on two DGEB tasks: with Foldseek's structural retrieval as
a base classifier and a small-LM majority fallback, that ceiling is
reachable on a CPU laptop. The DGEB suite does evaluate nucleotide-track
foundation models on other tasks (MIBIG Classification, EC DNA
Classification, ribosomal and operonic tasks); the two AA-only
classification tasks in this work are not part of that nucleotide-track
benchmark.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Function prediction from protein sequence.}
Methods such as DeepFRI \citep{gligorijevic2021deepfri} use graph
convolutional networks on AlphaFold-derived contact maps, and CLEAN
\citep{yu2023clean} contrasts EC-class prototypes against an ESM
backbone. These methods score well on benchmarks where train and test
share homology. The DGEB benchmark suite \citep{westroberts2024dgeb} was
introduced specifically to test what happens once the homology
shortcut is removed.

\paragraph{Structural retrieval.}
Foldseek \citep{vankempen2024foldseek} encodes each residue's local
3D environment as a token in a 20-letter alphabet (3Di) and reuses
sequence-search machinery (Smith--Waterman, profile HMMs) on those
tokens. It is several orders of magnitude faster than classical
structural aligners such as TM-align \citep{zhang2005tmalign} or Dali
\citep{holm1993dali}, at comparable accuracy on the standard benchmarks
reported by its authors. DGEB does not evaluate Foldseek.

\paragraph{Protein language models.}
ESM2 \citep{lin2023esm2} is a family of masked-language-model
transformers pretrained on UniRef sequences, with sizes from 8M to 15B
parameters. ProstT5 \citep{heinzinger2024prostt5} is a
bilingual T5 model trained jointly on amino-acid sequences and on
Foldseek 3Di tokens, giving a single model that handles both
modalities. The Nucleotide Transformer family
\citep{dallatorre2024nt} and Evo \citep{nguyen2024evo} are nucleotide
foundation models pretrained on genomic DNA; both are evaluated on
DGEB's nucleotide-track tasks (MIBIG Classification, EC DNA
Classification, ribosomal and operonic tasks), but not on the two AA
classification tasks examined in this work.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Data}

This study uses the DGEB \texttt{convergent\_\allowbreak enzymes}
dataset ($2000$ train, $400$ test, $400$ classes) and
\texttt{ec\_\allowbreak classification} dataset
($512$ train, $128$ test, $128$ classes). For each UniProt ID
in either split, the AlphaFold predicted structure
\citep{jumper2021alphafold,varadi2024afdb} is downloaded via the EBI
API. Coverage is $98.3\%$ on Convergent Enzymes
($2360 / 2400$ proteins) and $94.8\%$ on EC Classification
($607 / 640$); the remaining proteins have no entry in AlphaFold DB.

\subsection{Pipeline}

The pipeline has three components.

\paragraph{Structural retrieval (Foldseek).}
A Foldseek database is built from the train set, and
\texttt{easy-search} is run with default parameters
(\texttt{-s 9.5}, \texttt{-e 10}, \texttt{--max-seqs 50}) and the
standard 3Di scoring matrix. For each test query, the top-1 hit's EC
label is returned.
Foldseek's per-hit alignment probability \texttt{prob} serves as a
confidence signal.

\paragraph{Amino-acid language-model fallbacks.}
Every protein is embedded with four LMs, each treated as a separate
classifier head: ESM2-35M, ESM2-150M, and ESM2-3B
\citep{lin2023esm2}, and ProstT5 in its AA mode
\citep{heinzinger2024prostt5}. Embeddings are produced by mean-pooling
the encoder's last hidden state over residue positions, excluding the
\texttt{[CLS]} and \texttt{[EOS]} special tokens. Sequences longer than
the model's context (1022 tokens for ESM2-3B, 1024 for the others) are
truncated. For each LM, a regularised logistic regression ($C = 1.0$)
is fit on the standardised training embeddings and used to predict on
the test set.

\paragraph{Confidence-gated ensemble.}
The final prediction for each test query is
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}(x) = \begin{cases}
y^{\text{Foldseek}}_{1}(x) & \text{if } p_{\text{Foldseek}}(x) \geq \tau \\
\text{plurality}\bigl\{\hat{y}_{m}(x) : m \in \mathcal{M}\bigr\} & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{M}$ is a task-specific fallback ensemble. For
Convergent Enzymes, the best-tested configuration is the 3-LM
majority $\mathcal{M}_{\text{CE}} = \{\text{ESM2-3B}, \text{ProstT5},
\text{ESM2-150M}\}$. For EC Classification, where the LMs are
individually stronger (see Section~\ref{sec:per-lm}), the best-tested
configuration is the 4-LM majority
$\mathcal{M}_{\text{EC}} = \{\text{ESM2-3B}, \text{ProstT5},
\text{ESM2-150M}, \text{ESM2-35M}\}$. The confidence threshold $\tau$
is swept in $\{0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9\}$. The best configuration on
Convergent Enzymes uses $\tau = 0.9$; on EC Classification any
$\tau \leq 0.5$ ties at the maximum. Ties in the majority vote are
broken deterministically by Python's \texttt{collections.Counter}
insertion order (i.e., the first model that voted for the tied label).

\subsection{Compute}

The full pipeline runs on a 4-core Intel i5-11300H laptop with 16~GB RAM
and no CUDA GPU. The two large language-model embeddings (ESM2-3B and
ProstT5) are run on a free Google Colab T4 GPU session (approximately
one hour total: roughly $60$ to $75$ minutes for ESM2-3B including the
model download, plus about $10$ minutes for ProstT5). Foldseek runs in
a WSL2 Ubuntu environment on the same laptop. Total wall-clock for the
full Convergent Enzymes pipeline, including all negative-result
experiments, is approximately 8 hours.

On EC Classification (homology preserved, $128$ classes), the best
configuration is Foldseek with the 4-LM majority fallback gated at
$\tau \leq 0.5$. The recipe is insensitive to threshold choice:
$\tau \in \{0.3, 0.5\}$ both yield $F1 = 0.7305$ exactly; $\tau = 0.7$
gives $0.7227$ and $\tau = 0.9$ gives $0.7214$. Foldseek top-1 alone
on this task scores $F1 = 0.663$.

On Convergent Enzymes (homology removed, $400$ classes), the best
configuration uses the 3-LM majority fallback. The gain over the
ESM2-3B baseline is small ($+0.002$ F1, corresponding to roughly one
correctly predicted test query out of $400$), but the configuration
matches or exceeds the baseline across all four Foldseek-confidence
thresholds tested, with F1 values $0.265, 0.266, 0.267, 0.267$ for
$\tau \in \{0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9\}$. At the best threshold $\tau = 0.9$,
$91$ of the $400$ test queries fall to the LM majority fallback
(either because Foldseek's top-1 confidence is below $0.9$, or
because Foldseek returned no hit at all for that query, which is the
case for $13$ queries). Foldseek top-1 alone reaches $F1 = 0.238$.

A BCa bootstrap over the test queries ($10{,}000$ resamples) gives
$95\%$ CIs of $[0.62, 0.79]$ on EC and $[0.21, 0.29]$ on Convergent
Enzymes; the DGEB ESM2-3B baseline lies inside both, so neither point
gain is significant at the $95\%$ level. The comparison is unpaired
(DGEB's per-query predictions are unavailable) and hence conservative;
on EC, $95\%$ of resamples still exceed the baseline, a suggestive but
not conclusive gain. The contribution is therefore that a
training-free, laptop-scale pipeline \emph{matches} a 3B-parameter AA
foundation model on both tasks, not that it beats it.

A few facts worth noting from Table~\ref{tab:ablation}.

\begin{itemize}
\item The reimplementation of ESM2-3B alone scores $0.188$, against
      $0.265$ reported by DGEB on the same model, reflecting a
      different downstream probe protocol (DGEB's evaluation code was
      not replicated here). The ensemble nonetheless reaches
      $F1 = 0.267$, because Foldseek dominates in the confident regime
      and the weaker LM fallback only operates on the low-confidence
      remainder.
\item Hand-crafted features that describe the protein as a whole
      (secondary structure percentages, contact-map summaries,
      pocket descriptors) cap out at $0.037$. Foldseek, which compares
      \emph{per-residue local environments} against a database, reaches
      more than $6\times$ this number with zero training. The discriminative
      signal for convergent enzymes is in the local 3D environment of
      individual residues, not in the global fold.
\item Discrete motif counting on 3Di tokens never exceeds
      $F1 = 0.073$ in the experiments reported here. Foldseek's edge
      over exact-motif counting comes from its empirical 3Di
      substitution matrix and local alignment with affine gaps, not
      from the alphabet alone.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Per-LM contribution and ensemble robustness}
\label{sec:per-lm}

Within the ensemble, the per-LM contributions differ sharply between
tasks. On Convergent Enzymes the LMs are weak alone (ESM2-35M $0.161$,
ESM2-150M $0.139$, ESM2-3B $0.188$, ProstT5 $0.171$) and serve only as
a tie-breaking fallback; their value is in their disagreement patterns,
not individual strength. On EC the LMs are much stronger alone
(ESM2-3B $0.592$, ProstT5 $0.548$, ESM2-35M $0.428$, ESM2-150M $0.403$)
and contribute directly, especially where Foldseek finds several close
hits.

The ensemble result is insensitive to the confidence threshold $\tau$
on both tasks: the four thresholds span $0.721$ to $0.730$ on EC
Classification (Section~\ref{sec:results-main}) and $0.265$ to $0.267$
on Convergent Enzymes. The recipe is therefore not sensitive to
hyperparameter cherry-picking.

Three observations.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Foldseek's per-hit probability is by far the strongest signal of
      ensemble difficulty ($p = 3 \times 10^{-5}$), confirming that the
      confidence-gating strategy is well calibrated.
\item Hard queries are nominally longer ($p = 0.02$ on CDS length,
      $p = 0.05$ on protein length), though neither survives Bonferroni
      correction for the five properties tested. One candidate
      mechanism: longer proteins have more scaffolding per catalytic
      residue, so the catalytic signal is more diluted in any
      whole-protein measure (Foldseek included).
\item Hard queries trend toward higher CDS GC content ($p = 0.09$, not
      significant). DGEB does not evaluate nucleotide-track models on
      this task, so whether DNA-context models exploit codon-level
      signals here cannot be tested directly; the trend is reported as
      a property of the AA-track hard set.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Negative results}

\paragraph{Pocket-geometry features.}
fpocket \citep{leguilloux2009fpocket} detects $5$--$15$ surface
cavities per AlphaFold structure. The top-3 pockets' geometric
descriptors (volume, druggability score, hydrophobicity, polarity,
alpha-sphere statistics) plus the amino-acid composition of the top-1
pocket's lining residues were extracted, yielding $81$ features per
protein. The best classifier on these features reaches $F1 = 0.019$
on Convergent Enzymes, only modestly above random. fpocket detects
generic cavities, not catalytic sites; the top-1 pocket by score is
rarely the actual active site. Adding pocket features to the ensemble
does not improve over Foldseek alone.

\paragraph{Discrete motif counting on 3Di tokens.}
Three motif-counting variants on the 3Di sequences of the training
proteins were tested: linear k-mers (best $F1 = 0.073$ at $k = 4$,
in-class-fraction $\geq 3/5$, log-enrichment $\geq \log_2 10$);
spatial pairs and triples of 3Di letters at residues with $|i-j| \geq 6$
and $C_\alpha\text{--}C_\alpha \leq 9$~\AA{} (best $F1 = 0.016$);
and joint amino-acid$\times$3Di spatial motifs (best $F1 = 0.035$).
None approaches Foldseek's $F1 = 0.238$. This is attributed to
Foldseek's local alignment with the empirically trained 3Di
substitution matrix; exact motif matching, even in a $400$-letter
joint alphabet, cannot replicate that.

\paragraph{Nucleotide Transformer v1 ensembling.}
An attempt was made to add a nucleotide-track signal to the ensemble
by embedding CDS DNA with the Nucleotide Transformer
\citep{dallatorre2024nt}. NT v2 (the most recent variant) failed to
load under recent \texttt{transformers} versions on Colab; its custom
remote-code config exposes attributes (\texttt{rope\_theta},
\texttt{is\_decoder}, \dots) that newer \texttt{transformers}
releases do not provide as defaults. The fallback was NT v1 2.5B
Multispecies, which loads cleanly. In this pipeline (LogReg on
mean-pooled embeddings) it scores $F1 = 0.008$ alone, and adding it
to the majority vote drops the ensemble from $F1 = 0.267$ to
$F1 = 0.264$ on Convergent Enzymes. DGEB does not benchmark either NT
variant on Convergent Enzymes Classification, so this negative result
is not directly comparable to any DGEB nucleotide-track number; it is
reported only to document the attempt.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Why structural retrieval works on these tasks.}
On Convergent Enzymes, by construction, no train/test pair within a
class shares significant sequence similarity, so the discriminative
signal cannot be raw sequence. That Foldseek alone, comparing
per-residue local 3D environments via the 3Di alphabet
\citep{vankempen2024foldseek}, reaches $F1 = 0.238$ without any
training (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}) is consistent with the
structural-and-mechanistic-convergence paradigm of
\citet{riziotis2025paradigms}: in a substantial share of their curated
cases, active-site geometry is preserved across sequence and fold
divergence. The cases where convergence is mechanistic-only or
reaction-only are plausibly the ones Foldseek misses; the small-LM
fallback closes the residual gap to the ESM2-3B ceiling.

\paragraph{Why the same recipe spans both tasks.}
On EC Classification, train and test share homology, so the nearest
structural neighbour is often near-identical and Foldseek
short-circuits the prediction; the higher EC score reflects that
easier regime. On Convergent Enzymes the neighbour is not necessarily
homologous but remains the best structural candidate, with the small-LM
majority filling in below the gating threshold. One recipe handles both
regimes without retuning.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The Convergent Enzymes win margin is small ($+0.002$ F1) and
      not by itself a strong claim. The two-task evidence rests on
      the EC gain plus the ability to reach the ESM2-3B ceiling on
      the harder task without training.
\item Only two of the eighteen DGEB tasks are evaluated. Generalisation
      to the broader DGEB suite (operonic pairs, retrieval, phylogeny)
      requires pipeline changes that the current code does not
      implement.
\item The ESM2-3B reimplementation here under-reproduces DGEB's
      published numbers on both tasks ($0.188$ vs $0.265$ on
      Convergent Enzymes, $0.592$ vs $0.680$ on EC), a
      downstream-protocol mismatch (DGEB's evaluation pipeline was not
      replicated). The ensemble result does not depend on closing this
      gap, as Foldseek dominates in the confident regime.
\item The nucleotide-modality track is not engaged. DGEB does not
      evaluate nucleotide-track models on either task here, so that
      comparison is out of scope; the NT v1 ensembling attempt is a
      documented negative result, not a contradiction of any DGEB
      number.
\item The pipeline consumes predicted 3D structure (AlphaFold) that
      the sequence-only ESM2-3B baseline does not use. The comparison
      therefore measures whether a training-free, structure-aware
      pipeline can substitute for a large sequence-only model, not a
      like-for-like model comparison; ``no training'' refers to the
      downstream pipeline, not the upstream structure predictor.
\item DGEB provides no validation split for these few-shot tasks, so
      the threshold $\tau$ and the fallback-ensemble composition are
      chosen on the test split. Every $\tau$ in the sweep clears the
      baseline on both tasks (Section~\ref{sec:per-lm}), which limits
      the effect, but the headline figures are best-over-sweep on the
      test set.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Future work.} Two natural directions. First, validate the
Foldseek-as-active-site mechanism against M-CSA
\citep{ribeiro2018mcsa} annotations: test whether Foldseek's
per-residue 3Di matches preferentially align catalytic residues to
catalytic residues. Second, extend the pipeline to the broader DGEB
suite, in particular the retrieval and clustering tasks where
structural similarity could contribute.

\section{Conclusion}

A training-free pipeline of Foldseek structural retrieval and a
confidence-gated small protein-LM majority fallback matches the DGEB
ESM2-3B baseline on EC Classification ($F1 = 0.730$ vs $0.680$) and on
the homology-removed Convergent Enzymes task ($F1 = 0.267$ vs $0.265$);
the baseline lies within the bootstrap $95\%$ CI of each. It uses no
task-specific training, runs on a CPU laptop with about one hour of
free Colab GPU time, and is fully reproducible from public data:
structural retrieval can substitute for a large sequence foundation
model on these tasks at a fraction of the cost.
